SHIP COMBAT

The Sixth Crew Member

Rules for fights between ships: crew stations, ship frames and modules, weapons, damage, and repairs.

00 / Foundations

Design Philosophy

Void Combat treats the crew's ship as a sixth member of the party. The characters pilot it, repair it, and upgrade it over the course of the campaign. The system has three design goals:

◈ STATION-BASED PLAY

Each player character takes a Station during ship combat and acts with their own traits and abilities, so every player has a decision to make each round.

⌬ SHARED JEOPARDY

Damage to the ship threatens the whole crew. Losing the ship strands the characters, which gives decisions about risk and power real weight.

◇ BUILD WHAT YOU FLY

Each ship combines a Frame (the hull) with Modules (installed equipment). Two Corsair-class ships with different Modules play differently.

The system uses the same Duality Dice, Signal/Static economy, damage thresholds, and Evasion as the rest of Voidheart. Players who know the core rules already know most of what this chapter uses.

01 / The Ship Sheet

Ship Anatomy

Every ship has the following stats recorded on a shared Ship Sheet.

◈ HULL POINTS

Hull Points represent the ship's structural integrity. Like a character's Hit Points, you mark Hull when you take damage above a threshold. When all Hull Points are marked, the ship is Crippled (see Critical Systems). Ships have 8–26 Hull Points depending on their Frame.

✺ SYSTEM STRESS

System Stress represents strain placed on the ship's power grid, drives, and critical systems through combat damage and crew-driven overclocking. It is marked in a track separate from Hull. When all System Stress slots are filled, the ship suffers a Cascade Failure (see Critical Systems). Most ships have 6 System Stress slots. Some Frames and Modules modify this. Crews clear System Stress with Engineering actions during combat and with repairs afterward (see Ship Repairs).

⌬ EVASION

A ship's Evasion reflects how hard it is for enemy ships and weapons to get a clean lock on it. When an enemy attacks your ship, the GM rolls against your ship's Evasion. Base Evasion is set by the ship's Frame. The Helm station and certain Modules can modify it.

◈ ARMOR SCORE & ARMOR SLOTS

Ships have an Armor Score and a corresponding number of Armor Slots, identical in function to character armor. When your ship takes damage, a crew member at the Engineering Station can mark an Armor Slot to reduce the severity of incoming damage by one threshold: Severe → Major → Minor → None. Marked Armor Slots are restored by a Minor Repair or a Full Overhaul (see Ship Repairs).

✺ POWER CAPACITY

Power represents the ship's available energy output. Each active Module has a Power Cost. The total Power Cost of all active Modules cannot exceed the ship's Power Capacity. If a Module is deactivated during combat (by damage or choice), its Power is freed immediately. Reactivating a deactivated Module requires an Engineering action.

◇ SPEED

Speed determines how freely the ship moves through range bands. Frames have a Speed from 1 to 5, and Modules can raise it to a maximum of 6.

  • With a Helm action, the ship moves up to its Speed in range bands.
  • At Speed 3 or higher, the ship can also move one range band each round without using a Helm action.

◈ WEAPON MOUNTS

Each ship has a number of Weapon Mounts. A Weapon Module takes up both a Weapon Module slot and a Weapon Mount, and draws Power like any other Module. A weapon from the Ship Weapons table takes up only a Weapon Mount and draws no Power.

03 / The Crew Economy

The Ship Signal Pool

Ship combat uses a shared Ship Signal Pool in addition to each character's personal Signal.

  • When any player character rolls with Signal during ship combat, they may choose to add 1 Signal to the Ship Signal Pool instead of their personal Signal (to a maximum of 6).
  • Powered Station Actions draw from this pool.
  • At the end of each ship combat encounter, the Ship Signal Pool resets to 0.

Signal banked early in a fight pays for Powered Actions later in it.

Stations & Action Types

At the beginning of any ship combat encounter, each player character chooses a Station. A ship only needs one crew member at each Station to function, but two characters can share a Station, with one acting and the other helping. You make Station Action rolls using your character's traits. Experiences can apply at the GM's discretion. Each station has three action types:

◈ STANDARD ACTIONS

Always available on your turn. No resource cost.

◈ POWERED ACTIONS

Cost Signal from the Ship Signal Pool and have stronger effects.

✺ OVERCLOCKED ACTIONS

Cost System Stress instead of Signal. These are the strongest actions.

⚓ HELM

Trait: Agility · Instinct

The pilot manages the ship's position, speed, and evasive maneuvers.

▸ STANDARD ACTIONS

Evasive Pattern

Describe how you're moving the ship. Roll Agility or Instinct against the GM's Difficulty.

  • Success with Signal: Raise Evasion by +3 until your next turn. Gain Signal.
  • Success with Static: Raise Evasion by +2 until your next turn.
  • Failure: The ship is momentarily exposed. The GM gains Static.
Reposition

Move the ship up to your Speed rating in range bands. No roll is required unless the ship moves through a hazard or contested space; in that case, roll Agility against the GM's Difficulty.

Lock Trajectory

You stop maneuvering to give Gunnery a stable firing platform. Until your next turn, the ship's Evasion decreases by −2, but all Gunnery attacks gain advantage.

◈ POWERED ACTIONS

Hard BurnCost: 1 Ship Signal

Push the drives beyond rated output. Move the ship up to double its Speed rating instantly. Mark 1 System Stress.

Threading the NeedleCost: 2 Ship Signal

Describe a dangerous maneuver. Roll Agility against Difficulty 18. On a success, no enemy can target your ship until your next turn. On a failure, the ship takes Minor damage from collision hazards.

✺ OVERCLOCKED ACTIONS

Ramming CourseMark 2 System Stress

Make an Agility Roll against the target's Evasion. On a success, deal 2d12 + your Strength modifier physical damage to the target. Your ship also takes 1d8 physical damage. On a failure, the GM may impose a consequence.

🎯 GUNNERY

Trait: Finesse · Knowledge

The gunner operates the ship's weapon systems.

▸ STANDARD ACTIONS

Weapons Lock

Select a target within range of one of your installed ship weapons and fire. Roll Finesse or Knowledge against the target's Evasion.

  • Success with Signal: Full damage. Gain Signal.
  • Success with Static: Deal damage, then choose: the GM gains Static, or mark 1 System Stress.
  • Failure: Miss. The GM gains Static.
Suppressing Fire

Pick a range band and direction. Any ship attempting to move through that zone must succeed on a Difficulty 14 roll or mark 1 System Stress.

Target Assessment

Roll Knowledge against Difficulty 12. On a success, learn one fact about the target ship. With Signal, learn two.

◈ POWERED ACTIONS

Fire SolutionCost: 1 Ship Signal

Your next Weapons Lock action this turn gains advantage on the attack roll.

SalvoCost: 2 Ship Signal

Fire multiple weapons simultaneously. Make a Weapons Lock roll against each enemy ship within range (or the same target twice). Each attack is resolved separately.

✺ OVERCLOCKED ACTIONS

Overcharge ShotMark 1 System Stress

Make a Weapons Lock roll. On a success, double one damage die in the roll. On a failure, the weapon overheats and can't fire next round unless the Engineering station spends an action to cool it.

Target SystemsMark 2 System Stress

Make a Finesse Roll against Difficulty 18. On a success, choose one:

  • Destroy or disable one of the enemy ship's active Modules.
  • Reduce the enemy ship's Evasion by −3 until their Engineering station repairs it.
  • Your next successful hit against that ship this round deals double damage.

🔧 ENGINEERING

Trait: Knowledge · Strength

The engineer manages damage control, power routing, and system repairs.

▸ STANDARD ACTIONS

Patch Job

Roll Knowledge against Difficulty 12.

  • Success with Signal: Clear 1 Hull Point or 2 System Stress. Gain Signal.
  • Success with Static: Clear 1 Hull Point or 1 System Stress.
  • Failure: The GM chooses: mark 1 additional System Stress, or you can't take an Engineering action next round.
Armor Hold

When the ship takes damage, use your reaction to mark an Armor Slot and reduce the damage severity by one threshold. Happens before Hull Points are marked.

Power Reroute

Deactivate one Module (freeing its Power) and immediately activate another Module that was previously offline. No roll required.

◈ POWERED ACTIONS

Emergency ShieldingCost: 1 Ship Signal

Until the start of your next turn, the ship's Armor Score increases by +2.

Systems PurgeCost: 2 Ship Signal

Choose one: Clear 2 Hull Points, or clear all System Stress. If you clear Hull Points this way, mark 1 System Stress. If you clear System Stress this way, the ship's Power Capacity drops by 2 until your next long rest.

✺ OVERCLOCKED ACTIONS

Burn EverythingMark 2 System Stress

Choose one effect:

  • All Gunnery attacks this round deal an extra 1d12 damage.
  • The ship's Evasion increases by +5 until your next turn.
  • Clear 3 Hull Points immediately.
Emergency RestartMark 3 System Stress

Roll Knowledge against Difficulty 18. On a success, a destroyed Module functions again until the end of the encounter (after which it requires proper repair). On a failure, the attempt fails and you can't try again this encounter.

📡 COMMS & ECM

Trait: Knowledge · Presence

The Comms officer runs electronic warfare, sensor operations, and communications.

▸ STANDARD ACTIONS

Deep Scan

Roll Knowledge against Difficulty 12.

  • Success with Signal: Ask the GM two questions about any ship or object in sensor range.
  • Success with Static: Ask one question.
  • Failure: Your active scan pings their sensors. The GM gains Static.

Example questions: their Hull/Stress, hidden Modules, next likely move, who they're targeting, anyone aboard who might surrender.

Sensor Wash

Until your next turn, any enemy using Target Assessment or Deep Scan against your ship gets no useful information.

Open Channel

Hail another ship or contact. The default action for any communication during ship combat. Roll Presence for social outcomes.

◈ POWERED ACTIONS

ECM PulseCost: 1 Ship Signal

Roll Knowledge against Difficulty 14. On a success, one Module on the target ship is deactivated until their Engineering station repairs it. On Signal, choose which Module.

Ghost SignalCost: 2 Ship Signal

Until the start of your next turn, the enemy must succeed on a Difficulty 16 Knowledge Roll to target your ship at all. On a failure, their attack automatically misses.

✺ OVERCLOCKED ACTIONS

Hard BreachMark 2 System Stress

Make a Knowledge Roll against Difficulty 18. On a success, you gain full access to the target ship's sensor feed until they purge it (requires their Comms station to spend a Standard Action). While breached, you know their Hull, System Stress, active Modules, and next Station action choices.

🎖️ COMMAND

Trait: Presence

The commander doesn't pilot or fire. They coordinate the other Stations and keep the crew steady.

▸ STANDARD ACTIONS

Direct

Issue precise orders to one crew member at a Station. They gain advantage on their next roll. No roll required.

Read the Fight

Roll Presence against Difficulty 12. On a success, tell one allied crew member what action the GM is most likely to take this round (the GM must confirm or deny). On Signal, predict two possible GM actions.

Steady the Crew

One crew member of your choice clears 1 Stress. If you describe a particularly inspiring moment, spend 1 personal Signal to let them clear 2 Stress instead.

◈ POWERED ACTIONS

RallyCost: 1 personal Signal

Spend 1 of your own Signal to add 2 Signal to the Ship Signal Pool. No roll is required. Once per round.

Coordinated StrikeCost: 2 Ship Signal

Choose a crew member at another Station. They immediately take one Standard Action, in addition to their normal turn.

✺ OVERCLOCKED ACTIONS

All HandsMark 2 System Stress

Every crew member immediately takes one Standard Action at their Station, out of turn order and in addition to their normal turn. All these actions are resolved simultaneously.

04 / Round Structure

Combat Flow

Ground combat follows the spotlight. Void Combat uses a fixed turn order instead, so that every Station acts each round. Each round has five steps:

  1. Establish Range. At the start of combat, the GM places all ships at their starting range bands relative to each other.
  2. Determine Turn Order. At the start of combat, each crew member makes an Instinct Roll; the total is their Initiative. NPC ships act at Initiative 12, and Skirmishers act at 16.
  3. Player Turns. On a PC's turn, they take their Station Action. Some Station Actions use personal Signal/Static. Some feed the Ship Signal Pool.
  4. GM Turns. Enemy ships act on their Initiative count. Attacks roll against the player ship's Evasion.
  5. Round End. Check for Critical Systems conditions. Clear any "until your next turn" effects.

06 / When Things Break

Critical Systems

When something catastrophic happens to your ship, roll on the Critical Systems Table. This occurs when:

  • Your ship marks its last Hull Point.
  • Your ship fills all System Stress slots (Cascade Failure).

Roll 1d12 and apply the result. Each result complicates the fight without ending it.

d12Critical SystemEffect
1Drive FailureSpeed drops to 1. Engineering Standard Action to restore.
2Weapon Mount DestroyedOne weapon mount is offline until repaired at port.
3Targeting Array DownAll Gunnery rolls are made without advantage until repaired (Engineering action).
4Hull BreachOne section is venting atmosphere. Crew without suits must mark Stress each round they remain aboard.
5Power SurgeMark 2 additional System Stress immediately. One Module deactivates.
6Navigation FailureHelm cannot use Powered or Overclocked Actions until repaired.
7Sensor Array ScrambledComms Station loses all Powered and Overclocked actions until repaired.
8Armor BreachAll Armor Slots are marked until the ship reaches a port or dock.
9Emergency LockdownThe ship's AI initiates emergency protocols. All Station Actions this round are at Difficulty +4.
10Crew InjuryOne crew member (GM's choice or random) takes Major damage directly, bypassing the ship's Hull.
11Core InstabilityMark the Core Unstable condition. If the ship takes Severe damage again before it is repaired, roll on this table twice.
12She's Still FlyingNothing critical breaks. Gain 2 Ship Signal immediately.

Ending Ship Combat

Victory: The enemy ships are destroyed, disabled, or fled.

Disengage: A ship can attempt to disengage by moving to Deep Range. Requires a successful Helm action (Difficulty 14) and costs 2 System Stress.

Surrender / Boarding: A ship can broadcast surrender. The GM decides how the other side responds.

Crippled: When a ship marks all Hull Points, it is Crippled: it has no thrust, weapons, or shields until it is repaired.

10 / The Opposition

Building NPC Opposition

With fifteen player Frames and many Module combinations, building a custom enemy ship for every encounter isn't practical. Player Frames within a tier are balanced for durability, so build opposition to the crew's ship tier rather than to their exact hull and Modules. NPC ships use a reduced stat line: no Module slots, no build step, and one special feature.

◈ NPC SHIP STAT LINE

Tier · Role · Hull · Evasion · Attack · Damage (avg) · Attacks/round · [1 Special] · Threat Value

  • Hull is a pool of damage. Subtract each hit's damage from it; NPC ships have no damage thresholds, Armor, or System Stress. At 0 Hull the ship is disabled.
  • Attack: NPC ships attack with +2 at Tier 1, +4 at Tier 2, and +6 at Tier 3. Capitals add another +1. The GM rolls a d20 plus this bonus against the target ship's Evasion.
  • Initiative: NPC ships act at Initiative 12; Skirmishers act at 16.
  • Converting player effects: each System Stress an effect would make an NPC ship mark deals 2 damage to its Hull instead. An effect that triggers on Severe damage triggers against an NPC ship when a single hit deals at least 16 damage at Tier 1, 18 at Tier 2, or 21 at Tier 3.
  • Optionally, give a Heavy or Capital an "Overload" feature that triggers at half Hull.

These numbers are tuned so that a focused crew disables a Skirmisher in about 1 round, a Line ship in about 2, and a Heavy in 3 to 4. NPC damage is set to reach the thresholds of player ships of the same tier.

✺ ENCOUNTER BUDGET

Spend a Threat Value budget of ≈ 1.5 × the number of players, using NPC ships of the crew's ship tier. For a 4-player crew, TV 6 makes a fair fight, TV 8 a hard one, and TV 4 an easy one. To field a swarm, drop one tier and double the number of ships. A single Capital of the crew's tier (TV 6) is a full encounter on its own.

Feature & Module Impact

Frame Features and strong Modules don't appear in the NPC stat line. To account for them, classify each one by what it affects:

ClassWhat it doesAdjustment
DurabilityRaises effective survivability the stat line can't see (Cloak, damage saves, damage redirection, hull/threshold/armor modules).The fight runs longer: +1 TV for each such source the crew relies on.
Offense / TempoShortens the fight; the crew is effectively stronger (advantage engines, armor-ignoring or apex weapons, extra actions).Enemies go down faster: +1 TV for each such source the crew relies on.
Force MultiplierBuffs other allied ships (Flagship, Force Multiplier, Field Support).No effect with a single ship. Adjust only if the crew fields more than one ship.

The base NPC numbers already assume a competently-equipped crew, so ordinary loadouts need no correction. For each standout offensive or defensive feature/module the crew genuinely leans on, add +1 TV. Adjust the budget rather than rebuilding the enemy ships.

15 / Between the Fights

Ship Repairs

Ships are repaired during downtime in port or dock. These mirror character downtime moves.

⌬ MINOR REPAIR

Short rest equivalent: a few hours in dock

Choose one:

  • Clear up to 4 Hull Points.
  • Clear all System Stress.
  • Restore all marked Armor Slots.
  • Repair one Critical Systems condition.

◈ FULL OVERHAUL

Long rest equivalent: a full day or more

Clear all Hull Points, System Stress, Armor Slots, and Critical Systems conditions. A Full Overhaul also allows the crew to swap or install one Module (if they have the parts and credits).

16 / Improvement

Upgrading Your Ship

Ships improve through Module Installation and, at major story milestones, Frame Upgrades.

✺ INSTALLING NEW MODULES

Costs credits (set by GM, typically 500–2000 credits per tier of module) and requires access to a properly equipped port or shipyard. Installation usually takes one day per Module tier.

◇ FRAME UPGRADES

Acquiring a Tier 2 or Tier 3 Frame is a story event. The crew must find the ship, earn it, steal it, or commission it. Tier 2 hulls become available around Campaign Level 3, and Tier 3 hulls around Level 6. A Frame Upgrade resets the Module Slots to the new Frame's configuration.

17 / Optional Rule

Ship Experience

If the GM wants to give ships a sense of growth independent of Modules, ships can accumulate Flight Time, a narrative currency the GM awards after significant encounters. At 3 Flight Time, the crew can permanently improve one of the following ship stats by the listed amount:

  • +1 Evasion
  • +2 to one Damage Threshold
  • +1 Hull Point
  • +1 Power Capacity
  • +1 System Stress slot
