LINEAGES

Fourteen Ways to Be Born

Your lineage is who you are at the cellular level — physicality, biology, and the deep evolutionary history that gives your body its shape. Each lineage grants two features. Choose one, then pair it with a Community to complete your heritage.

Names listed in capitals are the Voidheart designation. The smaller italicized name beneath each is the species this lineage was reskinned from in the original system — useful when looking up additional flavor or sourcebook material.

AEVARI

Elf — Galactic Standard “Aevari”

Aevari are tall, slender humanoids with angular features and sensory organs acutely attuned to psionic frequencies. Their ears are long and tapered — a shape that grows more pronounced with age, the tips eventually beginning to curl. Heights typically range from six to six and a half feet. All Aevari share one universal trait: the ability to drop into a deep meditative trance in place of conventional sleep, achieving full cognitive restoration in a fraction of the time required by other species.

The Aevari homeworld is a place of intense biological wildness and unusual void-physics. Living within that environment across millennia has left a permanent mark — many Aevari develop a resonance form: bioluminescent patterns spreading across the skin, eyes that bleed to solid silver or depthless black, hair that drifts as if suspended in zero-g. Resonance forms shift in response to environment, emotional state, and psionic focus. Aevari live for approximately 350 years.

QUICK REACTIONS

Mark a Stress to gain advantage on a reaction roll.

VOID TRANCE

During a rest, you can drop into a deep meditative state to choose an additional downtime move.

CARAPAX

Galapa

Carapax are heavily armored humanoids whose bodies are encased in dense, segmented carapaces — broad-backed, chitinous shells that cover the torso and extend in articulated plates across the shoulders, forearms, and upper legs. No two Carapax look exactly alike; their carapace structure draws from a wide spectrum of natural forms. They range from four to six feet in height and move with a deliberate, planted weight that makes them appear immovable even when they're not trying to be.

What every Carapax shares is the decoration. From adolescence, the carapace becomes a canvas — carvings record lineage and battle history; inlaid alloys and bioluminescent compounds mark rank and covenant; pigment worked deep into the chitin announces allegiances and debts. Their cultural memory is one of formation and mutual defense: the phalanx instinct runs deeper than tactics — it is closer to reflex. Carapax live for approximately 150 years.

CARAPACE

Gain a bonus to your damage thresholds equal to your Proficiency.

RETRACT

Mark a Stress to pull your limbs beneath your carapace. While retracted, you have resistance to physical damage, disadvantage on action rolls, and you can't move.

CLANK

Clank — synthetic constructed beings

Clanks are sentient synthetic beings constructed from a vast array of materials — alloy plating, composite polymers, salvaged components, and custom fabrication. They can resemble humanoids, animals, or entirely utilitarian forms. Like organic beings, their bodies come in a wide array of sizes. Because of their bespoke construction, many clanks have highly specialized physical configurations: manipulator claws, mobility rigs, integrated sensor arrays, or built-in weapon hardpoints.

A clank's operational lifespan extends as long as they can acquire or fabricate replacement parts, making their physical form effectively indefinite. That said, their cognitive substrate is subject to the effects of time; long-running processes degrade, memory architectures fragment, and a clank who has outlived their original power source is never quite the same.

PURPOSEFUL DESIGN

Decide who built you and for what purpose. At character creation, choose one of your Experiences that best aligns with this purpose and gain a permanent +1 bonus to it.

EFFICIENT

When you take a short rest, you can choose a long rest move instead of a short rest move.

DRAKARI

Drakona

Drakari are large, scaled reptilians of humanoid build — a species whose biology reads, to most across the galaxy, as somewhere between apex predator and geological force. They range from five to seven feet in height, with dense musculature, long sharp teeth, and thick plated scales that resist both kinetic and energy-based damage. New teeth grow continuously throughout a Drakari's approximately 350-year lifespan.

The most distinctive feature of the Drakari is their breath gland — an internal biological organ capable of projecting superheated plasma, corrosive acid, or cryo-compressed gas depending on lineage. This trait passes reliably through generations, though rare individuals express a different output than the rest of their family. In some cultures, a Drakari whose breath diverges from their bloodline is treated as a sign.

SCALES

Your scales act as natural protection. When you would take Severe damage, mark a Stress to mark 1 fewer Hit Points.

BREATH GLAND

Choose an energy type for your breath (such as plasma, acid, or cryo). You can use this breath against a target or group within Very Close range, treating it as an Instinct weapon that deals d8 energy damage using your Proficiency.

FORGEBORN

Dwarf — “Forgekin” in some sectors

Forgeborn are compact, densely built humanoids whose biology bears the unmistakable mark of their homeworld — a tectonically violent planet of deep magma tunnels, crushing pressure gradients, and near-zero natural light. Their average height ranges from four to five and a half feet, but what they lack in stature they compensate for in mass: Forgeborn are disproportionately broad, with bone density and musculature evolved across millennia of high-gravity, subterranean labor.

Their skin carries a faintly granular texture — the result of elevated keratin concentration and natural silicate deposits in the dermis. This makes Forgeborn integument significantly tougher and more heat-resistant than most humanoid tissue. The same property allows them to embed ore fragments, gemstones, or conductive filaments directly into the skin as adornment. Forgeborn live to approximately 250 years.

THICK SKIN

When you take Minor damage, you can mark 2 Stress instead of marking a Hit Point.

INCREASED FORTITUDE

Spend 3 Signal to halve incoming physical damage.

GOLIATH

Firbolg

Goliaths are the kind of species that recalibrates a room the moment they walk into it. Tall, broad, and built with a density of musculature that makes even augmented humanoids look slight by comparison, they range from five and a half to seven feet in height. A Goliath at rest looks like a threat. A Goliath in motion is one.

Their faces tend toward broad and angular, with heavy brow ridges, wide-set eyes, and a flattened nasal structure that gives some members of the species a faint bovine read. Skin tones run from deep earth tones to pale grays, with visible subcutaneous ridging across the brow and shoulders. Goliaths live for approximately 150 years and maintain their physical capability well into the final decades. Their reputation as combatants is biomechanical reality — the force a Goliath generates in motion is not something most armor was designed to handle.

CHARGE

When you succeed on an Agility Roll to move from Far or Very Far range into Melee range with one or more targets, mark a Stress to deal 1d12 physical damage to all targets within Melee range.

UNSHAKABLE

When you would mark a Stress, roll a d6. On a result of 6, don't mark it.

MOTE

Faerie

Motes are beings of cohered energy — luminous, shifting, and only loosely constrained by the concept of a body. Their physical forms exist across a broad spectrum: some Motes cohere into tall, roughly humanoid silhouettes of concentrated light; others prefer to compress into a small, dense point of energy — quick, near-invisible, and difficult to track. Most fall somewhere between and move fluidly along that spectrum.

Within a humanoid configuration, Motes may manifest what appear to be features: compound eye-clusters of refracted light, luminescent patterns that shift with mood, appendages that trail like heat-shimmer, or a lantern-bright core visible through their outer silhouette. They are deeply attuned to psionic frequencies and void phenomena. They live for approximately 50 years before their energy begins to disperse.

LUCKBENDER

Once per session, after you or a willing ally within Close range makes an action roll, you can spend 3 Signal to reroll the Duality Dice.

ENERGY FORM

You can fly freely. While flying, mark a Stress after an adversary makes an attack against you to gain a +2 bonus to your Evasion against that attack.

MYCELID

Fungril

Mycelid are not what most species would call a singular being. A Mycelid is a colony — a distributed fungal consciousness that inhabits and grows through organic matter, threading itself through whatever body it currently calls home in dense networks of pale filament. Some Mycelid grow their own biological forms from birth. Others take residence in prepared hosts — cultivated organic shells, donated remains, or bodies consented to by their previous occupants. The galaxy finds this practice unsettling. Mycelid find the galaxy's discomfort with death puzzling.

Mycelid communicate within their own lineage through mycelial arrays — chemical and electrical signals broadcast through fungal networks embedded in local environments: soil, walls, ship hulls, the soft tissue of anything recently dead. Their colonies can persist for approximately 300 years, though some network clusters are rumored to be far older.

MYCELIAL ARRAY

Make an Instinct Roll (12) to extend your network into the local environment. On a success, you can communicate with other Mycelid across any distance.

DEATH CONNECTION

While touching a corpse that died recently, you can mark a Stress to extract one memory from the corpse related to a specific emotion or sensation of your choice.

SCRAPPER

Goblin

Scrappers are small humanoids built for environments that would overwhelm most species — the underbelly of stations, the pressurized crawlways of working ships, the salvage fields and industrial margins where nobody else bothers to look. Their large, forward-set eyes gather light efficiently in near-total darkness, and their membranous ears are tuned to pick up threat frequencies through hull vibration, atmospheric hiss, and the particular quality of silence that precedes something going wrong. A typical Scrapper stands between three and four feet tall. In a fight, a corridor, or a crowd, they are very rarely where you last saw them.

Scrappers use precise ear positioning as a nonverbal language — a system that developed in the noisy, comms-jammed conditions of industrial work. Those unfamiliar with the system miss entire conversations happening in plain sight. The name "Scrapper" began as something others called them. Most have since decided it fits. Scrappers live for approximately 100 years.

SUREFOOTED

You ignore disadvantage on Agility Rolls.

DANGER SENSE

Once per rest, mark a Stress to force an adversary to reroll an attack against you or an ally within Very Close range.

STRIDE

Halfling

Stride are small, fur-covered humanoids most recognizable by their wide, rounded ears and their tendency to look younger than they are. They range from three to four feet in height, with dense soft fur in colors from pale cream and warm amber to deep russet and near-black, often with patterning across the face and shoulders. A Stride's appearance changes little between adulthood and old age — something other species find either charming or unsettling depending on context. They live for approximately 150 years.

No Stride homeworld exists, or if it once did, no living Stride can point to it on a chart. They are a people of the void — traveling in flotillas of salvaged, inherited, and purpose-built vessels. A Stride can pick a familiar crewmate's footstep out of the ambient noise of a working ship, detect pressure shifts that precede a hull breach, and navigate by gravitational gradient and stellar drift with an instinctive accuracy that baffles most sensor arrays. They call this sense the pull.

COMMUNAL LUCK

At the start of each session, everyone in your party gains a Signal.

INTERNAL COMPASS

When you roll a 1 on your Signal Die, you can reroll it.

TERRAN

Human

Terrans are most easily recognized by their dexterous hands, rounded ears, and bodies built for endurance. Their average height ranges from just under five feet to about six and a half feet, with a wide variety of builds spanning quite broad to lithe and everything between. Terrans are physically adaptable and adjust to harsh environments — low gravity, high pressure, thin atmosphere, extreme cold — with relative ease, a trait that made them effective colonists and has since scattered them across more systems than any other species can reliably count. Terrans live to about 100 years.

HIGH STAMINA

Gain an additional Stress slot at character creation.

ADAPTABILITY

When you fail a roll that utilized one of your Experiences, you can mark a Stress to reroll.

THERYN

Faun

Theryn are tall, angular humanoids built around proportionately long limbs that give them a distinctive silhouette at any range. Their faces tend toward angular planes, with wide-set eyes and pupils that contract to a distinctive horizontal slit. Heights range from four to six and a half feet, though their stance shifts dramatically based on posture; a crouched Theryn and a fully upright one can appear to be entirely different creatures.

The most immediately striking feature is their horn structure — heavy, deeply ridged, and prominently forward-set. Growth continues throughout their approximately 225-year lifespan, and older individuals carry configurations that speak to decades of survival: swept-back crowns, branching laterals, broad arcing pairs worn smooth at the tips from use. Theryn are capable of explosive bursts of movement, and their hooves deliver strikes with enough force to dent hull plating. They are built for terrain that would slow most species down.

VAULTING STRIDE

You can leap anywhere within Close range as though you were using normal movement, allowing you to vault obstacles, jump across gaps, or scale barriers with ease.

KICK

When you succeed on an attack against a target within Melee range, mark a Stress to kick yourself off them, dealing an extra 2d6 damage and knocking back either yourself or the target to Very Close range.

TITAN

Giant

Titans are the descendants of a now-fractured Terran empire that, at the height of its expansion, turned its considerable scientific resources inward — splicing genetic material from dozens of conquered and allied species into the human genome in pursuit of the perfect void-faring soldier. The project succeeded, after a fashion. The modifications took hold, propagated through generations, and produced something that is no longer quite human. The empire that created them is largely gone. The Titans remain.

They are large — six and a half to eight and a half feet tall, broad-shouldered, with elongated arms that betray the non-human genetics woven through their lineage. Average lifespan runs to approximately 75 years — a cost of the unstable genome. The instability is the defining fact of Titan biology: as a Titan ages, suppressed traits from the source species begin to assert themselves — additional sensory organs developing in the skull, dermal structures hardening or shifting in texture, limb proportions changing, bioluminescent patches emerging or fading.

ENDURANCE

Gain an additional Hit Point slot at character creation.

REACH

Treat any weapon, ability, or other feature that has a Melee range as though it has a Very Close range instead.

VOIDSPAWN

Infernis

Voidspawn are what happens when the deep void leaves a mark that doesn't fade — not through attunement or study, but through something more fundamental. They are a species shaped by direct biological exposure to the farthest, strangest reaches of space, where the laws of matter thin and something older bleeds through. To look at a Voidspawn is to see a being that is partly of this galaxy and partly of whatever lies beyond it.

Their most immediately distinctive traits are their void-matter growths: dense crystalline protrusions erupting from the skull, brow, and shoulders — not bone exactly, but something close to it, shot through with faint bioluminescence that pulses with emotional state. Their coloring tends toward extremes: deep purples, pitch black, cold silver, void blue, occasionally broken by the uncanny contrast of pale near-white. Their eyes carry no visible iris or pupil in the conventional sense — to look into them is to look into a section of open space. Voidspawn live for approximately 350 years, and their void-nature becomes more pronounced with age.

FEARLESS

When you roll with Static, you can mark 2 Stress to change it into a roll with Signal instead.

VOID VISAGE

You have advantage on rolls to intimidate hostile creatures.

▾ Heritage Part Two

Choose Your Community

Your second heritage choice is your community — the worlds, fleets, or stations where you came of age. Its values, customs, and pressures shape who you became. Each community grants a feature you can use during play. Pick one of the nine, and read its name through a sci-fi lens: a Driftborne grew up in a generational fleet, a Spireborne came from privileged orbital station hierarchy, a Shadeborne knows what the underdocks really run on.

◈

CRESTBORNE

was Ridgeborne

From high places — orbital ridges, station crests, mountain peaks of frontier worlds.

◈

DRIFTBORNE

was Seaborne

Generational fleets, deep-void communities, those who came of age between systems.

◈

HOLLOWBORNE

was Underborne

Beneath the surface — tunnel cities, asteroid interiors, abandoned subway warrens.

◈

LOREBORNE

was Loreborne

Communities organized around the keeping and sharing of knowledge — archives, libraries, research stations.

◈

ORDERBORNE

was Orderborne

Disciplined communities bound by code, oath, or law — orders, militaries, monastic colonies.

◈

REACHBORNE

was Wildborne

Far-frontier worlds, edge-of-charted-space settlements, places where nature still pushes back.

◈

SHADEBORNE

was Slyborne

The underdocks, the gray markets, the parts of every station where the cameras don't reach.

◈

SPIREBORNE

was Highborne

Privileged orbital hierarchies, corporate towers, the families that own the stations.

◈

WANDERBORNE

was Wanderborne

No fixed home — raised in transit, drifting between communities, never quite of any of them.

Community features work identically to their Daggerheart originals — consult the source community cards for mechanics, and re-flavor them through a sci-fi lens at your table.

▾ A Note on Languages

Galactic Standard

In Voidheart, you're not asked to pick specific languages for your character. The game assumes everyone speaks a common tongue — often called Galactic Standard, transmitted through translator implants, fleet pidgins, or simple lingua franca — and that gestural sign-cant is widely understood across cultures, ship communities, and species. If you'd like specific regional or species-bound languages in your campaign, discuss options with your table.
